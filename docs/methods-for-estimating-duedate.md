The "Method for Estimating the Due Date" published by the Committee on Obstetric Practice ACOG (Committee Opinion #700, May 2017) emphasizes the critical importance of accurate pregnancy dating for improving outcomes, research, and public health.

Key recommendations and methods for estimating the due date (EDD) include:

* **Early Determination and Documentation:** As soon as data from the last menstrual period (LMP), the first accurate ultrasound examination, or both are available, the gestational age and EDD should be determined, discussed with the patient, and clearly documented in the medical record. Subsequent changes to the EDD should be rare and also clearly documented.
* **First-Trimester Ultrasound Accuracy:** Ultrasound measurement of the embryo or fetus in the first trimester (up to and including 13 weeks 6 days of gestation) is considered the most accurate method to establish or confirm gestational age.
* **Assisted Reproductive Technology (ART):** If the pregnancy resulted from ART, the ART-derived gestational age should be used to assign the EDD (e.g., based on the age of the embryo and date of transfer for in vitro fertilization).
* **LMP-Based Dating:** The EDD can be estimated by adding 280 days (9 months and 7 days) to the first day of the LMP, often using Naegele's Rule. However, this method relies on accurate maternal recall, assumes regular 28-day cycles, and can be less precise than ultrasound.
* **Redating with Ultrasound:** The LMP-determined EDD should be revised and redated to the ultrasound-determined EDD if there is a significant discrepancy, with specific guidelines for different gestational age ranges:
    * **First-trimester ultrasound (using Crown-Rump Length - CRL):**
        * More than 5 days difference before 9 weeks 0 days of gestation.
        * More than 7 days difference between 9 weeks 0 days and 13 weeks 6 days of gestation.
    * **Second- and third-trimester ultrasound (using composite of Biparietal Diameter - BPD, Head Circumference - HC, Abdominal Circumference - AC, and Femur Length - FL):**
        * More than 7 days difference between 14 weeks 0 days and 15 weeks 6 days of gestation.
        * More than 10 days difference between 16 weeks 0 days and 21 weeks 6 days of gestation.
        * More than 14 days difference between 22 weeks 0 days and 27 weeks 6 days of gestation.
        * More than 21 days difference at or after 28 weeks of gestation.
* **Suboptimally Dated Pregnancies:** A pregnancy without an ultrasound examination that confirms or revises the EDD before 22 weeks 0 days of gestational age should be considered suboptimally dated.
* **Best Obstetric Estimate:** The gestational age at delivery, determined by the methods outlined in this document, represents the best obstetric estimate for clinical care and should be recorded on the birth certificate. For research and surveillance, this best obstetric estimate should be used over LMP-alone estimates.
