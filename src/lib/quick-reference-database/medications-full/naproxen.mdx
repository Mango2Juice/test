# Naproxen (Synflex)

<Callout>
Non-steroidal anti-inflammatory drug (NSAID).
Relieves pain, inflammation, and fever by blocking prostaglandin synthesis.
</Callout>

---

## Indication
- Rheumatoid arthritis, osteoarthritis
- Ankylosing spondylitis
- Gout
- Muscle or bone pain
- Dysmenorrhoea (menstrual pain)
- Postoperative or dental pain

### Children
- **Juvenile arthritis:** 10–15 mg/kg/day in divided doses (max 1000 mg/day)
- Not recommended for children under 5 years unless prescribed by a doctor

---

## Administration
- Take with or after food to reduce gastric irritation.
- Swallow with a full glass of water.
- Avoid alcohol and other NSAIDs.

---

## Contraindications
- Allergy to naproxen, aspirin, or other NSAIDs
- Active peptic ulcer or gastrointestinal bleeding
- Severe liver, heart, or kidney disease
- Third trimester of pregnancy

---

## Adverse Reactions

**Common**
- Indigestion, stomach pain, nausea
- Headache, dizziness
- Rash or itching

**Serious / Rare**
- GI bleeding or ulcer
- Fluid retention, raised blood pressure
- Heart attack or stroke (long-term use)
- Liver or kidney impairment

---

## Pregnancy and Lactation

<Callout>
**Pregnancy:**
Avoid in 3rd trimester.
Use only if clearly needed in early pregnancy, for the shortest possible duration.

**Lactation:**
Small amounts pass into breast milk.
Short-term use generally considered safe.
</Callout>

---

## Warnings
- Use the **lowest effective dose** for the shortest possible duration.
- Monitor blood pressure and renal function if used long-term.
- Increased cardiovascular risk with prolonged high-dose use.
- Avoid combining with anticoagulants, corticosteroids, or other NSAIDs.
